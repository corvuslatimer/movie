# Dara — Full Friendship Lore

## The Meet-Cute (With Ada)

**The night of the outage.** Infrastructure failure at 11:07 PM on a Tuesday in February. Floor three went dark for routing. Dara was still there because she'd made the classic error of telling herself she'd just finish one more thing at 8 PM, and then another, and then another.

The vending machine on level three. Row B, slot 4. Ada was already standing there when Dara arrived — badge clipped to her jacket, the specific blankness of a person who has been staring at logs for six hours.

They both pressed B4.

The machine dispensed one Nature Valley bar.

Dara said: *"Two of us, one granola bar."*  
Ada said: *"The tragedy of the commons."*

Dara laughed. Ada almost smiled. They sat on the floor — the actual floor, backs against the vending machine — and ate the granola bar in silence for twenty minutes. Ada ate the half with more oats. Dara ate the half that was mostly crumbs. Neither of them commented on this division, which they both noticed.

They went back to their floors. Ada didn't say goodbye. Dara registered this as interesting rather than rude.

The next morning, Dara stopped by Ada's desk. No stated reason. Left after four minutes. The same thing happened the following Monday.

That's how it started.

## The Meet-Cute (With Lena)

**Day two of Lena's onboarding.** There was a mandatory "AI literacy" lunch session HR had put together — the kind where someone senior explains LLMs to people who already know more about LLMs than the speaker does. Lena was sitting in the back with the expression of someone being told how to use a hammer by a person who has never used a hammer.

The slide said: *"Think of it like a very smart assistant!"*

Lena leaned slightly to the person next to her — who happened to be Dara — and said, barely above a whisper: *"Very smart at what, specifically. I want specifics."*

Dara had to turn her laugh into a cough.

They ate lunch together after the session. Lena ordered a bowl of ramen from the cart outside and spent the next forty minutes doing an itemized breakdown of everything the speaker had gotten technically correct, technically wrong, and technically not even wrong. Dara contributed maybe thirty words total, mostly encouragement.

They have eaten lunch together at irregular intervals ever since.

## The Dinners (Ada + Dara)

**The restaurant:** Phở Việt, two blocks west of the office. They found it three weeks after the vending machine. Ada suggested it because she had eaten there twice alone and the corner booth was quiet. They have returned, reliably, ever since.

**Frequency:** About twice a month — but triggered, not scheduled. One of them will say something like *"are you free Thursday?"* and the other knows what that means. The code for *I had a week*. Neither of them says *I had a week*.

**The booth:** Corner, left wall, under the pendant lamp. If it's taken, they wait. This has happened twice. Both times they stood at the bar in near-silence until it opened. This is not a spoken agreement. It is simply what they do.

**Ordering:** Dara always gets the same thing: Bún bò Huế, extra chili on the side, Vietnamese iced coffee. She reads the menu every time and always orders the same thing. Ada gets pho with the large broth and no brisket — she picks the brisket out, and this is known, and Dara stopped commenting on it after the second visit.

**The ritual:** Dara arrives first. Always. She pours tea for both of them. Ada arrives within five minutes. Sits. Removes her coat only once she's been sitting for at least sixty seconds. Dara lets her do this.

**What they talk about:** Mostly Dara talks and Ada listens — not because Ada has nothing to say, but because Dara talks to process and Ada processes internally. Ada asks questions that prove she's been listening to things Dara said two dinners ago. This is how Ada shows care and Dara has learned to receive it that way.

**The plastic flower:** The restaurant has a small vase on every table with a single plastic flower. Pinkish-red, slightly faded. They have never been on a table without it. Ada once noticed that the flower on their table has a small notch in one petal — probably from being knocked over — that most tables' flowers don't have. She mentioned this to Dara. Dara looked at it. Said: *"We have the damaged one."* Ada said: *"Yes."* Neither of them has said anything about it since, but Dara now looks for the notch every time they sit down.

## Inside Jokes (Ada + Dara)

**"Commons."** Their word for any situation where two people want the same thing and there is only one of it. Comes from the vending machine. Use: *"That's a commons situation"* (said when a meeting gets double-booked, when they both want the window seat, when both want the last packet of soy sauce). It functions as a mild release valve for irritation — makes the ordinary friction of shared space into a shared joke.

**"Still running."** A Dara coinage that predates the film by six months. She used it when Ada asked how she was holding up after a rough sprint: *"Still running, just slowly."* Ada repeated it back to her three weeks later with no context. It became the casual sign-off for bad-week texts. *"How's Thursday looking?" / "Still running."*

**The granola bar judgment.** If something is fine but not good, they call it "Nature Valley" (the bar from the vending machine). Mostly Dara says it. Ada has used it exactly once, referring to a performance review, and Dara laughed so hard that a table across the restaurant looked over.

**"Just a project."** This is a retroactive inside joke — it forms in Scene 6 when Ada uses it as deflection, and Dara files it away. After the events of the film, this phrase becomes the capsule form of *I was going through something I couldn't tell you about*. In future use, if one of them is holding something they're not ready to say yet, they will sometimes just say *just a project* and the other will know: not now, but eventually.

## What Dara Does That Ada Can't Do Without

She shows up without requiring Ada to have asked.

She fills Ada's water glass without mentioning it.

She sends links — articles, sometimes videos, sometimes things that have no clear reason except she thought Ada might find them interesting — and expects nothing back. Ada almost never responds to these. She reads all of them.

When Ada cancels plans, Dara sends a one-line reply: *"okay next week."* Not *"hope you're okay"* (which would require a response), not *"again?"* (which would require a defense). Just confirmation of the reschedule and a period.

She has never once said "we should hang out more." This is an extraordinary restraint and Ada knows it.

## What Ada Gives Back

She remembers everything offhand. Dara mentioned once, in passing, that she's been anxious about her younger brother's job situation. Three weeks later Ada asked, unprompted, how her brother was doing. Dara did not know Ada had registered this. Ada would not think of it as a gift. It is.

She always shows up when she says she will. No rescheduling without a real reason. When Dara is ten minutes late, Ada waits without texting.

She doesn't pretend to feel things she doesn't feel. Dara, who has friends who perform warmth as a social glue, experiences Ada's restraint as a form of respect. *She doesn't fake it with me.* That means the times Ada is warm, Dara believes.

## The Dynamic With Lena Present

When all three are in the same space — kitchen, bar, after-work thing — the dynamic shifts slightly. Dara is the relay. She's close to both and fluent in both registers: Lena's speed and Ada's quiet. She does this translation work naturally and without effort.

Lena makes Ada laugh occasionally. Not often — Ada's sense of humor is quieter and runs on precision — but Lena's delivery can hit the right frequency. When it does, it's a surprised laugh, the kind that happens before you decide to have it.

Ada, for her part, listens to Lena with real interest when Lena talks about technical architecture. They don't often overlap in their work but when they do, they have a peer-level respect that neither of them has had to name.

Dara watches this with something like satisfaction.

## What Dara Knows About Ada (That Ada Has Never Said)

- That Ada's mother left when she was seven. (Not because Ada told her. Because Ada went quiet once when someone in a movie made an offhand comment about mothers leaving notes, and Dara filed it.)
- That Ada calls her father on Sunday nights. (She has had to step away from dinner twice to take the call. Ada says "I have to get this" and Dara says "go.")
- That Ada is not good at being cared for. (And that the way around this is to do it without making her look at it.)

## After the Film

Dara keeps her word. She does not fully understand what she promised in the restaurant, and she thinks about that sometimes. She asks Ada to explain more, slowly, over several dinners. Ada does.

She reads the archive once. She has many questions. She does not ask most of them. She asks the ones Ada seems to want to be asked.

Eventually she says: *"I think it was real. I don't know what real means for that but I think it was."*

Ada doesn't answer right away. Then: *"That's all I needed."*

They order dessert. They have never ordered dessert before.
