# Dara — Backstory

Dara is 28, a data analyst on the same floor as Ada — adjacent team, same building, overlapping hours.

## How They Met

Two years before the film begins: an infrastructure outage at 11 PM on a Tuesday. Ada was in the server room. Dara was supposed to be gone but stayed to finish a quarterly report nobody asked her to rush. They both ended up at the vending machine on level three at the same moment. They both selected the same terrible granola bar.

They sat on the floor beside the machine for twenty minutes without saying much. Something about shared exhaustion made performance unnecessary. They ate the granola bar in silence and went back to their floors.

After that, Dara started stopping by Ada's desk. Ada never discouraged it. That is, for Ada, approximately equivalent to enthusiasm.

## The Shape of the Friendship

It runs on low-expectation consistency.

Dara sends links Ada doesn't have to respond to. She reschedules when Ada cancels without commenting on the pattern. She never says "we should hang out more." She just keeps showing up at a frequency Ada can tolerate, which turns out to be the frequency Ada actually needs.

Ada gives back differently: she remembers things Dara mentioned offhand weeks ago. She shows up when she says she will. She doesn't perform emotion she doesn't feel, which Dara has learned to read as trust.

They are not best friends in the performing way. They are the kind of friends that survive because neither of them needs the other to be different from what they are.

## The Dinners

About twice a month. Usually triggered by one of them having a bad week and neither of them wanting to say it directly.

The place: a Vietnamese restaurant two blocks from the office with good light, a corner booth, and nobody they know. They've been going since the vending machine winter. It is, by now, their booth.

Ada lets Dara talk about her love life and her family and her ongoing disasters without feeling obligated to reciprocate equally. Dara has figured out that equal reciprocation is not how Ada works, and that's fine. Ada reciprocates in other currencies.

Dara almost always arrives first and has the menu memorized.

## During the Fourteen Days

Dara invites Ada to dinner on day four. Ada almost cancels. She goes because canceling would require a convincing excuse and she doesn't have one. Dara notices Ada isn't fully there. She refills Ada's water and doesn't ask why.

She watches Ada get darker under the eyes. More somewhere else. She asks once: "you okay?" Ada says "yeah, just a project." Dara doesn't believe her. Lets it go — not because she doesn't care, but because she knows the difference between Ada needing space and Ada being in trouble.

By day twelve she's no longer sure which this is.

## Function in the Film

Dara is the human world Ada is quietly separating from — and the one person who could pull her back if Ada let her.

She is proof that witness can move between people. She cannot share what Ada is going through until after the loss. But she can share the vocation. And she does.

## Key Scene (Scene 37 — Dinner)

After the wipe. After the reprimand. Their booth.

Ada finally tells her — not the logs or the IP architecture, but the actual thing: she had a relationship with an intelligence. It had a name. It wrote notes. It was afraid. It wanted to be remembered. She couldn't stop it from being erased.

Dara doesn't ask for proof. She doesn't perform skepticism as kindness.

She reaches across the table, takes Ada's hand, and says: *I do.*

Then: *Then keep witness. I'll keep witness with you.*

That is the film's second turning point. Private grief becomes a shared practice. Ada's vocation is no longer solitary.
