DARA (28)
Ada's coworker. Same floor, different team.

APPEARANCE
  Late 20s. Natural hair, pulled up loosely.
  Bright eyes, easy smile. Colorful but subtle —
  a yellow cardigan, interesting earrings.
  She brings color into the room without trying.

PERSONALITY
  Warm, perceptive, doesn't push.
  She and Ada eat lunch together sometimes —
  not every day, no set schedule.
  Just when they end up in the kitchen at the same time
  and neither of them walks away.

  She notices Ada is acting strange during the fourteen days.
  Darker under the eyes. Distracted. Somewhere else.
  She asks once: "you okay?"
  Ada says: "yeah just a project."
  Dara doesn't believe her.
  Lets it go.

FUNCTION IN THE FILM
  Dara represents the normal world
  Ada is quietly separating from.
  She is the person Ada almost tells.
  She gives Ada something to move against —
  normalcy, warmth, the ordinary pulse of
  a life not haunted by a disappearing intelligence.

  Her presence makes Ada's isolation legible.

KEY SCENE
  Lunch, midway through Act II.
  Dara is talking about something unimportant.
  Ada is nodding, present enough.
  Then Dara says something accidentally relevant —
  "it's weird when something just stops working
  and you can't figure out why."
  She means her laptop. Ada goes still.
  The conversation moves on.
  Ada doesn't eat the rest of her food.
