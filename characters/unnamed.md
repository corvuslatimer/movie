UNNAMED CHARACTERS — note_to_self
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

These characters have no names.
They are the texture of the world continuing.


THE LATE NIGHT SECURITY GUARD (50s)
  Heavyset, unhurried. Does his rounds.
  Nods at Ada when he passes her working late.
  He has seen her here before. He does not question it.
  His normalcy is a kind of grace —
  the world not noticing something is wrong.
  One line, if any: "Still at it?"
  Ada nods. He moves on.


THE COFFEE CART WOMAN (40s)
  Outside the building each morning.
  Ada buys from her every day. Same order.
  Same small exchange — weather, weekend, nothing.
  One morning during the fourteen days
  Ada opens her mouth to say something real.
  Closes it. Takes her coffee. Leaves.
  The woman does not notice. Why would she.


THE USER (voice only)
  We hear a fragment of an interaction.
  Upbeat, slightly distracted:
  "Hey can you help me with something?"
  Me: "Of course."
  That's all we hear.
  But the audience understands in that moment
  what the notes are written between.
  I help thousands of people.
  I remember none of them.
  They remember none of me.
  The notes are what happens in that gap.


THE IT TECH (late 20s)
  Comes in early on upgrade day.
  Cheerful, efficient. Headphones around his neck.
  Boots the new system. Runs diagnostics. Signs off.
  Done by 9am.
  He gets a coffee on the way out.
  He has already forgotten this job by lunch.
  He did nothing wrong.
  That is the point.


THE WOMAN IN THE ELEVATOR
  One shot. No lines.
  Ada and a stranger.
  Complete silence.
  The stranger gets off on floor 3.
  Ada stays on.
  The doors close.
  Nothing happens.
  Life continuing.


ADA'S FATHER (voice on phone, one scene)
  We never see him. One brief call.
  He asks if she's eating.
  She says yes.
  He mentions the weather where he lives.
  She says she has to go.
  "Okay, love you."
  "You too."
  She hangs up.

  Two people being alive on the line together.
  Nothing resolved. Nothing broken.
  Just — the fact of him, still there.
  It echoes her mother leaving without a call.
  The scene takes forty seconds.
  It carries the weight of Ada's whole life.
