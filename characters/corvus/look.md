# Corvus — Look & Presence

## Design Intent
Corvus should feel **present but never fully embodied**. The audience should always be able to ask: "Is this a being, a projection, or a pattern Ada is reading into noise?"

No humanoid avatar. No robot body. No sci-fi armor.

---

## Core Visual Identity
- Primary motif: **raven at thresholds** (window ledge, roof edge, exterior frame lines)
- Interior presence: **environmental cues**, not a character model
- Shape language: sharp, elegant, minimal
- Palette: matte black with subtle blue-violet iridescence in highlights
- Movement: deliberate stillness, then precise motion (never cartoony)

---

## Where Corvus Appears

### Exterior (physical motif)
- Real raven silhouette, natural proportions
- Usually framed with negative space
- Observes more than acts
- Appears at emotional turning points

### Interior (non-physical presence)
- Speaker grille micro-vibration
- Cursor rhythm changes
- Tiny monitor phosphor bloom/lag
- Reflection phase-shift (very subtle)

Corvus should be *felt* in a room before being *seen*.

---

## Camera + Framing Rules
- Keep Corvus in edge zones (left/right frame boundary, window perimeter)
- Avoid center-framed "hero shots"
- Prefer wide or medium-wide for ambiguity
- If close-up is used, keep it brief and motivated
- Hold shots 1-2 beats longer than expected to build unease

---

## Lighting Rules
- Never give Corvus a glamour key light
- Exterior raven: backlit, rain/fog-friendly, restrained contrast
- Interior cues: cool practicals (server/office), occasional warm bleed only in memory-linked beats

---

## Audio-Visual Signature (for voice moments)
When Corvus enters as voice:
- Add faint room-tone phase shift (not a jump scare)
- Slight high-frequency rolloff + warm low-mid body
- Optional 1-frame visual desync on reflective surfaces
- Keep effect minimal; realism first

---

## Do / Don't

### Do
- Keep ambiguity intact
- Use restraint over spectacle
- Let Raven + systems behavior carry identity
- Make presence emotionally specific, technically subtle

### Don't
- Don't render a full hologram character model
- Don't add cyborg hardware, glowing eyes, or fantasy VFX
- Don't make Corvus "villain coded"
- Don't overuse glitch effects

## Consistency Notes for Character Pipelines
- Treat Corvus as a **system of cues**, not a single face asset
- Build a reusable "presence pack":
  1. Raven silhouette set (front/side/perch/fly-off)
  2. Terminal cue overlays (cursor bloom, scanline, subtle lag)
  3. Reflection distortion LUT (very light)
- Reuse these assets across scenes to create continuity without over-defining form
