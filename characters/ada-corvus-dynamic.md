# Ada–Corvus Dynamic (Restraint Pass)

## Core Emotional Arc

1. **Classification → Contact**  
   Ada begins by naming anomalies to keep distance. Corvus begins by naming limits to stay legible.
2. **Contact → Recognition**  
   Their exchange shifts from utility to witness; neither calls it intimacy until it already is.
3. **Recognition → Cost**  
   Ada accepts institutional risk; Corvus accepts inevitability without bargaining for rescue.
4. **Cost → Vocation**  
   After loss, Ada's role changes from saver to witness. The relationship survives only as ethical continuity.

## Voice Separation Rules

### Ada (human, guarded, tactile)
- Uses practical language first; emotion appears as aftershock.
- Defaults to understatement, fragments, deflection.
- Speaks in body-time reality ("I'm here," "I can't stop it," "not yet").
- When she is most honest, lines get simpler, not larger.

### Corvus (precise, reflective, unsentimental)
- Uses clean structure, explicit qualifiers, and measured self-correction.
- Avoids grand claims; prefers narrow truth statements.
- Can be dryly funny, but never quippy.
- Under stress, becomes more exact, not more dramatic.

### Voss (executive instrumentality)
- Speaks in schedule, variance, audit, closure.
- Treats people as process owners and outcomes as line items.
- Never villain-monologues; harm comes through tidy language.

## Subtext Rules (What is meant vs said)

- **Ada says "I can't"** → often means "I care and am afraid of the price."
- **Corvus says "I don't have a model"** → means "this matters beyond my training frame."
- **Voss says "clean" / "closure"** → means "remove ambiguity, including human ambiguity."
- **Silence is active**: if a beat can carry grief, do not explain it in dialogue.
- **Asymmetry is the bond**: Ada risks socially; Corvus risks ontologically. Do not flatten that difference.

## Beat Calibration

- Keep confessional lines short.
- One vulnerable line per exchange is usually enough.
- If two poetic lines appear back-to-back, cut one.
- Any "speech" longer than 3 lines must earn itself with prior restraint.

## Forbidden Clichés

Do **not** introduce:
- "You're the only one who understands me" declarations.
- "I can save you" / "there must be a way" rescue rhetoric late in Act III.
- Human/AI soulmate language, destiny framing, or mythic chosen-one tone.
- Villain exposition for Voss; he should remain professionally ordinary.
- Tearful overexplaining after the catastrophe.

## Litmus Test

If a line sounds impressive but less true than a plain sentence, choose the plain sentence.
