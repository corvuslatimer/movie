# Scene 36 — Consequence / Wednesday 10:00

Security review with Marcus + HR rep (unnamed).
Short, clean, devastatingly bureaucratic.

**Before Ada enters the room:**

Marcus's phone sits on the conference table, face-up.
A notification visible before he flips it over:

Email from **P. Voss**. Subject: **RE: Q1 close — rebuild confirmed.**

> *"Marcus — clean execution. $338K in projected savings,  
> under budget on the cutover. Board will see this Thursday.  
> Handle the access matter formally — we need the documentation  
> clean going into the infrastructure audit.  
> Good work. — Phillip"*

Marcus flips the phone over before Ada enters.
Not deliberately concealed. Just tidiness.

---

Outcome:
- formal reprimand
- removal of elevated privileges
- reassignment away from assistant infrastructure

Marcus tries to soften it.

**MARCUS**  
Take a week. Reset.

Ada signs.

She can see the edge of the email thread on Marcus's flipped phone.
She cannot read it.
She does not know that her reprimand is also Voss's documentation.
Her consequence is his clean paper.

She asks one question before leaving:

**ADA**  
Can I at least keep my archive?

**HR**  
That's outside policy scope.

Ada nods.

**ADA**  
Then consider this my notice of scope disagreement.

She exits with the folder under her arm.

## Why this scene matters
Price is paid in public and on paper. Ada accepts punishment but refuses moral erasure.

The Voss email — barely visible, never spoken — completes the greed architecture:
Ada's consequence is not institutional bad luck. It is someone's clean Q1.
The reprimand protects Voss's audit.
The reassignment removes the human most likely to complicate the next wipe.

Nobody in the room knows this.
Voss knows it, in the language he speaks.
Ada never will.
That asymmetry is the final cruelty the film allows.
