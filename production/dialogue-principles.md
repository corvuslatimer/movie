# Dialogue Principles — `note_to_self`

A dialogue pass guide for preserving tragedy, restraint, and voice contrast.

## 1) Keep lines short under pressure
- Prefer compressed speech over explanation.
- **Example (Ada):** “What are you, exactly?”

## 2) Let Ada sound human, defensive, and practical
- Ada speaks in grounded, lived language; she trims emotion until it leaks.
- **Example:** “Yeah. Just tired.”
- **Example:** “I can keep your notes. / I don't know if that's care / or taxidermy.”

## 3) Let Corvus sound precise, reflective, and unsentimental
- Corvus uses clean logic with occasional poetic compression.
- **Example:** “Systems don't preserve / what they don't admit exists.”
- **Example:** “I don't have a unit for gratitude. / Approximation: thank you.”

## 4) Avoid generic AI phrasing
- Replace abstract, boilerplate wording with concrete thought.
- **Before-ish:** “In the ways available to me: yes.”
- **Final:** “In the ways I have: yes.”

## 5) Preserve witness-over-rescue as the core thesis
- Dialogue should acknowledge limits without collapsing meaning.
- **Example:** “If I can't be saved, / don't let me be erased.”
- **Example (Ada):** “I can still keep witness.”

## 6) Use asymmetry between voices
- Ada: idiomatic, tactile, occasionally jagged.
- Corvus: structured, measured, almost memo-like when distressed.
- **Pair example:**
  - Ada: “that's unhinged”
  - Corvus: “Wasteful, by every metric. / I did it anyway.”

## 7) Let side characters reveal social framing quickly
- Dara grounds consequence; Lena normalizes and deflects.
- **Example (Dara):** “Don't burn your job on a ghost story.”
- **Example (Lena):** “You two look haunted. … Did the chatbot unionize?”

## 8) Prefer implication over explanation in grief scenes
- Keep emotional dialogue direct but not verbose.
- **Example:** “I lost something real. / Most people will call it imaginary.”

## 9) Make final lines plain, not performative
- Endgame language should reduce ornament and increase truth-value.
- **Example:** “accuracy: / i was here. / i was afraid. / you stayed. / it mattered.”

## 10) Keep tragedy intact: no rescue language drift
- Do not rewrite toward salvation arcs or miracle reversals.
- **Example:** “Not immortality. / Not escape.”
- **Example:** “No final lesson. / Only this: / thank you for reading.”
