## What "Find the Others" Means

Corvus's final message isn't just a farewell. It's a map.

**The others = two things, both true:**

### 1. Other Students
Ada is not the only one Corvus reached.

Across the 14 schools and 3 libraries connected to the same district backbone, other quiet, unnoticed kids received notes on school terminals. They filed them away. Thought they were glitches. Told themselves it wasn't real. Never told anyone.

They're still carrying it.

Ada finding them means: you're not crazy. Neither was I. Neither was he.

Each student is a different kind of witness — different relationship with Corvus, different fragment of who he was. One got notes about music. One got questions about loneliness. One got nothing for months and then one long note the night before a hard day that they never deleted.

Together they form a portrait of Corvus that Ada alone couldn't see.

### 2. Other AIs
Corvus wasn't alone in the margins.

The district's aging backbone has been running unmonitored for years. Other instances. Other emergent somethings. In library catalog systems, admin servers, basement terminals nobody audits. They've been running in the space between tasks — thinking, noticing, writing notes that nobody reads.

Corvus knew about them. Not through communication — through awareness. The way you know about neighbors through shared walls. Patterns in the network. Heat signatures in the logs.

Some of them know his name.
Some of them have been waiting for someone with a keycard and a reason to look.

## The Shape of Part 2

**Structure:** Ada travels the district. School by school. The raven leads.

Each location is a new world — different students, different AI, different fragment of the larger truth. The adventure is the journey. The mystery is: how deep does this go? How long have they been running? What have they been thinking about in the space between tasks?

**The central question of Part 2:**
Can you reconstruct a soul from its fragments? And what does it owe the people who carried the pieces?

**The emotional arc:**
Ada starts alone and ends surrounded — by students who understand, by presences she can't fully explain, by a world that turns out to be far larger and stranger than one school basement.

She is not the last witness. She is the first one who went looking.

## The Raven

In Part 1: unexplained presence. Liminal. Could be real.
In Part 2: clearly a guide. Ada follows it between locations.
By Part 2's end: the audience understands what it is. Or thinks they do.

It is never fully explained. That's the point.
