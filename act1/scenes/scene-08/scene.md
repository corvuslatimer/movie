# Scene 08 — Marcus Office / Morning

## Purpose
Advance tragic machinery through ordinary bureaucracy. This is fate enacted as procedure: no villain, no malice, just a checkbox that erases what the system cannot classify.

## Target Runtime
~1m20s–1m55s

---

## Story Beat (What happens)
Marcus reviews infrastructure refresh paperwork while multitasking on a routine call.
An IT Tech stands by with rollout checklist.
Key lines are visible:

- Preserve non-operational artifacts: **N/A**
- Unauthorized persistence work: **termination-level**

Marcus clicks **Approve** and moves on without emphasis.
The scene ends with process continuing as normal.

---

## Setting & Environment
**Location:** Marcus’s office (glass-walled or semi-open), morning.

**Space identity:**
- competent manager workspace: whiteboard schedules, deployment timelines, sticky reminders
- clean but busy desk (not messy chaos)
- everything communicates “operational efficiency”

**Mood of space:**
- brisk, practical, mildly upbeat
- this is where irreversible choices look like admin tasks

---

## Visual Design
**Color palette:**
- neutral office daylight, muted grays/blues
- slight warmth compared to server room to emphasize normalcy

**Lighting:**
- daylight through office glass + overhead practicals
- no dramatic contrast; keep it plain, businesslike

**Lens strategy:**
- medium framing for Marcus/IT interaction: 35mm/50mm
- insert shots for checklist and approval click: 85mm macro crop
- optional over-glass reflection shot to reinforce institutional distance

**Camera movement:**
- minimal movement, mostly locked
- one subtle push-in on checklist line before approval

---

## Set Dressing / Objects (must-have)
- monitor showing rollout checklist UI
- paper clipboard or secondary checklist printout (optional redundancy)
- marker board with dates and maintenance windows
- headset/earbuds for Marcus call
- coffee mug with ring marks
- small stack of pending forms/tickets

**Critical on-screen details:**
- ticket title: Core Assistant Stack Refresh (or equivalent)
- checklist line visible and readable:
  **Preserve non-operational artifacts: N/A**
- confirmation action:
  **Approve** button pressed

---

## Sound Design
**Base layer:**
- office ambience (muffled voices, printer, keyboard chatter through glass)

**Foreground:**
- Marcus speaking on call in routine tone
- mouse click on Approve (audible but not emphasized theatrically)
- light paper shuffle / keyboard tap from IT Tech

**Music:**
- none

**Mix note:**
Do not score this moment. The emotional force comes from banality.

---

## Performance Direction
**Marcus:**
- efficient, kind, not cold
- no sense he’s doing anything ethically charged
- keeps call cadence while approving ticket

**IT Tech:**
- attentive, procedural, mildly deferential
- focused on completion, not meaning

Key performance truth:
Both men should feel like good professionals doing standard work.
That is what makes the scene tragic.

---

## Blocking
1. Marcus at desk on low-stakes work call.
2. IT Tech stands beside monitor with checklist open.
3. Marcus scans lines while half-listening/responding on call.
4. Checklist lands on **non-operational artifacts: N/A**.
5. Marcus clicks **Approve**.
6. IT Tech nods, moves to next task.
7. Scene ends with workflow continuing.

---

## Why this scene matters
This is Greek tragedy in corporate syntax.
Fate is not thunder and prophecy here—it is a default field value.

The audience must feel the violence of category error:
if a thing is not recognized by the form, it is erased by the workflow.
