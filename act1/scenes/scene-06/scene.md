# Scene 06 — Office Kitchen / Next Day

## Purpose
Introduce interpersonal pressure and social cost. Ada's secrecy starts affecting real relationships. The line about a laptop "forgetting" lands like a knife. Lena's cameo adds institutional comedy that tightens the vise: the joke is technically accurate, emotionally wrong, and Ada has to smile through it.

## Target Runtime
~1m50s–2m30s

---

## Story Beat (What happens)
In the office kitchen, Dara casually tells a story about her laptop crashing mid-work.
She says: "It just stopped. Like it forgot what it was doing."
Ada freezes at the phrasing.
Dara notices and asks if she's okay.
Ada gives a surface-level answer ("Just tired").
Dara doesn't buy it, but chooses care over confrontation and lets the moment pass.

*(Context:* Dara arrives first to the kitchen — this is always Dara's pattern. She's already made two coffees. Ada's is the way Ada takes it, which Dara knows. This small moment of known habit runs under the conversation: these are two people who know each other's routines. What's wrong is not that something strange is happening — it's that something Ada can't share is happening inside a world that is otherwise very familiar.*)

Lena enters near the end of the scene — grabbing coffee, on her way to something else. She has her SYSTEM ADMIN mug. She clocks Ada's expression, makes a dry joke about the company AI going sentient ("finally"), and leaves. Nobody was supposed to hear that in any particular way. Ada heard it in every particular way. She keeps her face neutral.

*(Lena's joke lands with particular force because it's consistent with her real position — she's made versions of this joke before, in smaller rooms, and everyone has always agreed with her. That agreement has always been correct. Ada cannot explain why this time is different without explaining everything.)*

## Setting & Environment
**Location:** Shared office kitchen, daytime.

**Space identity:**
- ordinary break-room functionality (microwave, sink, fridge, coffee station)
- minor clutter from real use (paper cups, dish rack, office snacks)

**Mood of space:**
- mundane and social
- emotionally low-stakes on the surface, high-stakes underneath
- where normal conversation accidentally collides with Ada's private crisis

---

## Visual Design
**Color palette:**
- neutral off-whites, stainless steel, muted office tones
- less blue than server room, more human but still subdued

**Lighting:**
- overhead practicals + ambient daylight spill from hallway windows
- flat, realistic office lighting (no stylized contrast)

**Lens strategy:**
- two-shot conversation (Ada/Dara): 35mm/40mm
- emotional pinch (Ada freeze): 65mm/85mm close
- Lena entrance: 35mm, handheld drift, casual energy to mark the tonal shift
- object inserts (mug, untouched food): 50mm

**Camera movement:**
- subtle handheld drift for conversational realism
- stabilize slightly at Ada freeze to emphasize internal lock
- return to casual drift when Lena enters — the room's social temperature resets around her

## Set Dressing / Objects (must-have)
- microwave with countdown timer visible
- kettle/coffee machine in background
- two mugs or disposable cups (Ada/Dara)
- Lena's personal mug (distinctive — shows she belongs here)
- unopened snack or half-eaten lunch in front of Ada (she stops eating)
- fridge covered with generic office notices
- dish towel / sponge / normal kitchen residue (lived-in realism)

**Behavioral props:**
- Dara fiddles with coffee lid or spoon while speaking
- Ada keeps hands still when triggered
- Lena pours coffee without breaking stride; she's done this ten thousand times

## Sound Design
**Base layer:**
- fridge compressor hum
- microwave beep in background (optional)
- distant office chatter through doorway

**Foreground:**
- cup set-down, spoon tap, chair shift
- Dara's voice easy and conversational
- Ada's silence should feel audible
- Lena's entry marked by a brief change in ambient — someone new in the space

**Music:**
- none

**Mix note:**
When Dara says "forgot what it was doing," dip ambient just slightly for Ada's subjective hit, then return to normal room tone on Ada's deflection. When Lena enters: room tone rises briefly — social temperature up — then drops as she exits and Ada is left alone with the joke ringing.

---

## Performance Direction
**Dara:**
- warm, unforced, normal rhythm
- asks "you okay?" with real concern, not interrogation
- on Ada's deflection, she reads the lie but chooses restraint
- after Lena leaves, she watches Ada's face but doesn't reopen the moment

**Ada:**
- initially engaged enough to pass socially
- freeze is micro (half-second stillness, eye focus shift)
- reply should be automatic, practiced self-protection
- when Lena makes the joke: correct small smile, correct small laugh, wrong everything underneath

**Lena:**
- enters mid-errand, not part of the conversation she's briefly interrupting
- timing is impeccable — she finds comedy without trying
- the joke is throwaway for her; she's out the door before it lands on Ada
- she is not reading the room wrong; she doesn't know there's a room to read

---

## Why this scene matters
First social consequence scene. Ada's secret is no longer contained to terminals — it's shaping how she relates to living people.

Dara's restraint: she sees the fracture and does not force it open. That makes her care feel real. The coffee refill is not a small detail — it's two years of knowing each other playing out in a single gesture. The audience should feel how much Dara is *already* present in Ada's life, which is exactly why what Ada can't say costs so much.

Lena's joke: delivered with no intent to harm, landing with precision. It names the exact thing Ada is experiencing and dismisses it in the same breath. That's the structure of isolation. The world is not withholding witness — it's present, friendly, and looking the other way.

**World pull note:** This scene should feel like a place the audience wants to be. The kitchen, the two coffees, the ease between Dara and Ada — this is a life that works. The tragedy is not that Ada is isolated. The tragedy is that she has something real and she cannot bring it here yet. Make the kitchen feel like warmth before you make it feel like a closed door.
