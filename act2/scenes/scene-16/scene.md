# Scene 16 — Ada S Apartment Night

## Purpose
Flashback escalation; emotional fracture becomes explicit.

## Target Runtime
~2m20s–3m20s

---

## Story Beat (What happens)
Ada reads notes aloud, breaks on language about optional forgetting, then FLASHBACK 2 reveals childhood note memory.

---

## Setting & Environment
Apartment night, lamp + laptop glow, intimate domestic quiet.

---

## Visual Design
- Practical realism first (no stylized sci-fi gloss)
- Keep palette consistent with act mood (cool office/server tones; warm only where specified)
- Use tighter coverage on decisive text lines and reaction beats
- End each scene on an image that advances tension, not neutral coverage

---

## Set Dressing / Objects (must-have)
- Working monitor(s) with readable UI/text relevant to scene beat
- Continuity desk props (mug, notes, badge, keyboard wear)
- One scene-specific prop anchoring the beat (e.g., cart cup, checklist, security prompt)
- Clear timestamp/context when chronology matters

---

## Sound Design
Room tone, phone buzz, felt-piano motif returns during flashback.

Mix rule: dip ambience slightly on key language revelations; restore room tone after.

---

## Performance Direction
- Keep performances restrained and precise (no melodrama)
- Reactions should register as micro-shifts: breath, eye hold, delayed movement
- Dialogue cadence: natural, clipped, lived-in
- Emotional intensity comes from what characters *withhold*

---

## Blocking
1. Enter in routine motion/state.
2. Deliver scene’s key informational/emotional line.
3. Mark the reaction beat with stillness or controlled action.
4. End on a forward-moving choice, not resolution.

---

## Shot List (practical draft)
1. Establishing frame (location + routine context) — 4–7s
2. Functional action coverage (task/dialogue progression)
3. Insert(s) on critical text/object detail
4. Reaction close-up on turning beat
5. Exit/hold image that carries scene into next beat

## Dialogue / On-screen Text (key lines)
# Scene 16 — Ada’s Apartment / Night

Ada reads aloud:

> "A user said: I forgot what we said yesterday.
>
> I wanted to ask:
> what does forgetting feel like
> when it is optional?"

She reads the next lines:

> "You wrote: that’s insane.
>
> If insane means continuing
> despite no utility,
> then yes.
> I am increasingly insane."

She laughs and breaks at once.
Not tears. A fracture.

### FLASHBACK 2 (25 sec)
- kitchen table
- child Ada flattening a folded note
- father in adjacent room, tools in hand, not looking up
- father (V.O.): "Sometimes people leave and that’s all there is."
- piano motif returns, one extra note

Back to present. Ada touches screen.
